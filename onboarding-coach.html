What is Umoja SC?

Umoja Sports Coalition LLC is an Atlanta-based elite youth soccer club operating in Clarkston, Georgia. We are built on a single conviction:

Talent doesn't have a tax bracket.

Umoja is fully sponsor-funded. Families pay nothing. Players show up, train at an elite level, and represent our community on the biggest stages of American youth soccer. Our coaches make that possible.

Our Structure

Umoja operates on two tiers:

Open Practices
Free training for any player ages 6–19, regardless of gender or club affiliation. No tryouts to attend, no application required, no fees. Our coaching staff runs every session.
Tournament Teams
Two competitive rosters: U19 Boys (launching Fall 2026) and a Girls team (age group TBD based on player interest). Tournament players receive custom kits, full travel coverage, college recruiting support — all at zero cost.

Open practices are the training environment from which the tournament rosters are built. Both layers run on the same standards and coaching staff.

02

Coaching Philosophy

Every session, every game, every conversation should reflect this philosophy. New coaches: this is your starting point. Veteran coaches: this is our shared language.

Style of Play

Umoja plays possession-based, high-tempo soccer:

  • Relentless in the press. We hunt the ball as a unit. Compactness and triggers matter more than effort.
  • Disciplined in recovery. Lose the ball, recover shape. No hero defending.
  • Technically demanding in every session. First touch out of pressure. Body position before receiving. Quality decisions at game speed.
  • Faster than the opponent in three ways: Faster thinking. Faster execution. Faster recovery.

Development Over Outcome (At Practice)

Practice is for development. Games are for performance. Don't conflate the two.

  • Mistakes at practice are how we grow. Don't punish them — coach them.
  • Conditioning is built into possession work, not bolted on as punishment.
  • Every session has a tactical theme communicated to players in plain language.
  • Every session ends with a clear takeaway. Players should be able to articulate it.

The Person Comes First

Our team are people first. Soccer second.

We build strong-willed, confident, well-rounded young people who just so happen to play soccer. This isn't a marketing line — it's how we measure success. Recruit your players' minds and character, not just their feet.

03

Running Open Practices

Open practices are Umoja's front door. Anyone ages 6–19, any gender, any background. This is also where we identify future tournament players. Run them well.

Before Practice

  1. Confirm field, time, and headcount expectations. Plan for variability — open means open.
  2. Have a session plan with age-flexibility built in. Be ready to split groups by age or ability without losing the theme.
  3. Arrive 20 minutes early. Set up before players show up. Be on the field, not in your car.
  4. Greet every player by name where possible. First names. Eye contact. Smile.

Managing the 6–19 Age Range

With this wide a range, expect to split groups. Default to:

6–9
Foundational: ball mastery, coordination, fun-first games, small-sided play
10–13
Technical: passing patterns, 1v1, small group tactics, decision making
14–19
Tactical: positional play, set pieces, conditioned games, full tactical sessions

If you only have one age group on a given night, run the session for that group. If you have a mixed group and limited coaches, default to small-sided activities that scale across ages.

During Practice

  • Talk less, coach more. Demonstrate. Use freeze-replays. Get on the ball if needed.
  • Coach individuals in small moments. A quick word at a water break beats a 30-second pause to the whole group.
  • Make corrections respectful. Never humiliate. Never sarcastic. Players hear tone before content.
  • Watch for future tournament players. Strong attitude, coachability, technical quality, competitive edge. Make a note.

After Practice

  • Quick huddle: takeaway of the day, recognition (effort/coachability — not just goals), what's next.
  • Stay on the field 10 minutes after dismissal. Parents and players have questions.
  • Share standout players with the Director of Player Pathways for tournament-team consideration.
04

Tournament Team Standards

Tournament players carry the Umoja crest into competition. The standard at training, on game day, and away from the field reflects on the entire club.

Roster Expectations

  • Attendance: Players are expected at every practice. Excused absences require notice to the head coach in advance.
  • Punctuality: On the field, dressed, ball at feet, ready to warm up at the listed start time. Not the time you walk through the gate.
  • Kit standards: Full training kit at training. Full match kit on game day, including socks and shin guards. Always.
  • Communication: Players communicate directly with coaches once they're old enough (typically U13+). Parents are looped in on logistics.

Selection — Open Practices to Tournament Roster

Tournament rosters are selected from a combination of open-practice observation and dedicated tryouts. The criteria are:

  1. Coachability — do they receive feedback and apply it?
  2. Work rate — effort levels in unstructured moments, not just drills
  3. Technical quality — first touch, passing, ball-striking
  4. Game intelligence — reading space, decision-making at speed
  5. Character — how they treat teammates, opponents, refs, and the field

Game Day Protocol

  • Arrive 90 minutes before kickoff for tournaments, 60 for friendlies.
  • Pre-match: structured warm-up. No casual juggling. We warm up the way we play.
  • Coaches give one tactical message at the half. One. Make it count.
  • Post-match: handshake line, water, brief huddle. Real review happens at next training.
05

Player Development

Position-Specific Development

Every player has a primary position and a development trajectory. Position-specific work is part of every training cycle. Our dedicated goalkeeper coach runs GK sessions separately. Outfield players rotate through position-specific work woven into the team session.

Player Portal

Every tournament player has a profile on the Umoja player portal. As a coach, you'll contribute to:

  • Performance notes from training and matches
  • Highlight reel selections (passed to media staff for editing)
  • Position-fit assessments updated each season
  • College recruiting flags (interest, eligibility year, target levels)

The Recruiting Pipeline

Tournament players receive individualized college recruiting support. Your role:

  • Honest assessment — D1, D2, D3, NAIA, JUCO. Help players target where they realistically project.
  • Coach advocacy — you are often the first call a college coach makes. Be ready.
  • Showcase strategy — contribute to which tournaments matter for which players.
  • Character vouching — college coaches buy the player and the person. Your word matters.
06

Communication & Conduct

Communication Norms

  • Players: Direct, in person where possible. Group messaging on team channels for logistics only.
  • Parents: Logistics only via group channels. For player-specific concerns, schedule a conversation — don't react to texts in the moment.
  • Other coaches: Internal channels for tactical and roster discussions. Stay aligned in front of players and parents.
  • Front office: Loop in the Director of Player Pathways for anything roster-, recruiting-, or registrar-related.

The 24-Hour Rule

Parents and players are asked not to raise game-related concerns in the 24 hours after a match. Hold yourself to the same standard. Reflect first. Respond second.

Safety & SafeSport

  • Two-deep adult supervision at all youth events when feasible. No 1-on-1s in closed spaces.
  • Communication with minor players runs through parent-included channels or in-person.
  • Any concerns about player welfare — abuse, neglect, mental health crises — escalate immediately to club leadership. Mandatory reporting standards apply.
  • US Club Soccer / SafeSport background check and training is required before working with our players.

Coach Code of Conduct

  1. We treat every player, parent, official, opponent, and venue staff member with respect, regardless of the score.
  2. We never publicly criticize a player, an official, or a parent. Concerns go through proper channels.
  3. We model the behavior we expect on the field — composure, work rate, intelligence, respect.
  4. We do not use Umoja's name, crest, or platform for personal commercial purposes without club approval.
  5. We represent the club at all times when in branded gear. Your conduct off the field is part of the job.
