01

The Model

Umoja Sports Coalition LLC is an elite youth soccer club operating on a model that is rare in American youth sports: families pay nothing. No registration fees. No tournament surcharges. No fundraising obligations. Nothing.

Talent doesn't have a tax bracket. That's the whole point.

How It Works

Umoja is funded entirely by corporate sponsors and community partners who deduct their investment as a marketing expense under IRC Section 162 — the same mechanism that funds professional jerseys, stadium naming rights, and major league advertising.

Sponsors get authentic community visibility and brand presence. Your player gets elite-level training, custom kits, tournament travel, and college recruiting support. Your family pays $0.

We are not a nonprofit. We are not a charity. We are not asking for donations. We're a soccer club that chose a different funding structure — and it works.

02

How Umoja Is Structured

Two Layers, One Standard

Open Practices
Free training for any player ages 6–19, regardless of gender, club affiliation, or experience. No application required. No commitment. Just come.
Tournament Teams
Selected rosters that travel and compete. Currently: U19 Boys (Fall 2026 launch) and a Girls team (age group TBD).

Most players start at open practices. From there, coaches identify players ready to compete at the tournament level and invite them to tryouts.

03

What's Covered

Whether your player is at open practice or on a tournament roster, the club covers:

  • Coaching — our full staff, including a dedicated goalkeeper coach
  • Field and training resources — venues, equipment, session materials
  • Custom kits and gear (tournament players) — match kits, training gear, outerwear, bag
  • Tournament travel (tournament players) — transportation, lodging, tournament fees
  • College recruiting support (tournament players) — individualized, including media production
  • Insurance and registration — US Club Soccer membership and supplemental coverage

What's Not Covered

The few things we ask you to handle:

  • Transportation to and from local practices and home games. Carpools happen organically; we don't organize them, but parents often do.
  • Personal cleats and shin guards. Match and training kit is provided; personal footwear is on you.
  • Player nutrition and hydration. We provide guidance, not groceries. Snacks and drinks at tournaments are on the family.
  • School and life. You're the parent. We're the coach. The line is clean.
04

Sideline Conduct

How you behave on the sideline is part of your player's experience.

Umoja parents are a critical part of the program. We ask you to operate by the same standards we ask of our players:

At Practice

  • Drop off, watch from a respectful distance, and let coaches coach. Avoid coaching from the sideline.
  • Questions about your player's development — schedule a conversation. Don't intercept the coach at pickup.
  • Stay out of the technical area unless invited.

At Games

  • Cheer your team, not against the opposition. Loud is fine. Negative is not.
  • Never address officials. Coaches handle officials. Period.
  • Don't coach your child from the sideline. It creates conflicting messages and breaks their focus.
  • Model what you want to see. Your player is watching you more than you think.

The 24-Hour Rule

If you have a concern about a game — playing time, tactics, your player's role — we ask you to wait 24 hours before raising it. Then schedule a conversation with the head coach. Don't text from the parking lot. Don't post on social media. Don't approach the coach as players are walking off the field.

Frequently Asked

Is this really free? What's the catch?

Yes, completely free, and there is no catch. The model is sponsor-funded — sponsors invest, your player benefits, the community thrives. No fundraising required, no fees later, no hidden costs.

How is this different from a scholarship?

Scholarships require families to prove financial need, often only cover part of the cost, and create a stigma. Umoja doesn't ask. Every player on our roster pays the same: nothing.

Will my player make a tournament team?

Tournament rosters are selected based on ability, work rate, coachability, and character. Coming to open practices is the best way to be considered. There are no guarantees — but every player gets a fair look.

Where do I sign up?

For open practices: just show up. For the U19 Boys tournament team: submit interest at apply.html. For the girls team: submit the interest form at girls-interest.html.

Who runs Umoja?

Umoja is led by Juliana Hill (Director of Player Pathways & Registrar), Head Coach Bosco Hakuzimana, Assistant Coach Alhaji Tambadu, and Goalkeeper Coach Gad Nkurunziza. The club is a Georgia LLC under the Full90Impact Enterprise umbrella.
