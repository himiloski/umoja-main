Ground

Umoja has never had a field of its own. This is how that changes.

Site search underway

We’re looking for home.

Enough room for the club we’re building — not just the club we are today.

Control the calendar. Build the club.

Umoja rents every hour it trains. Renting means training when someone else has a gap, turning down camps because there is nowhere to hold them, and paying for a facility the club will never own. The same piece of ground, owned, does all of this.

01PracticesOn our schedule, not the gaps in someone else’s
02Home gamesA ground the club actually plays at
03Summer campsWithout paying outside field rental
04School breaksWinter and holiday programming
05ClinicsPosition work, goalkeeping, small groups
06TryoutsHeld properly, on our own dates
07EventsTournaments the club hosts rather than travels to
08RentalsTrainers and clubs using capacity we are not

The same asset cuts a cost and creates a revenue line. Every hour Umoja does not rent is money kept, and every hour the club is not using the field is an hour someone else can. We are still modelling what that is worth, and we will publish it when the numbers are real rather than estimated.

The Brief

What we’re looking for.

A scouting report for a piece of land, not a fundraising target.

The site

Substantial usable acreage

Room for a mature campus rather than the minimum that fits today’s club. We would rather buy well once, further out if the economics are meaningfully better, than buy small twice.

The first build

Approximately two full-size fields, plus what makes them work

Parking, access and the essential infrastructure a working site needs. What we build first depends on what the land costs and what the site demands — a cheaper parcel means more soccer, a harder one means we start smaller.

The future

Room for more fields and facilities

Additional full-size fields and future training space as the club grows into them. We do not want to spend serious capital on ground that traps Umoja at two fields.

The job

A site that works six days a week

Not a field that sits empty between Saturdays.

Growing Home Ground

  • Additional full-size fields

Everything in outline is conceptual. It has no cost attached, no date and no commitment. The field surfaces are not decided either — what we build, and what we build it from, will be settled by the site we buy and the budget left after we buy it.

Recognition

Build your place in Home Ground.

Home Ground will be built by people who choose to put something permanent behind Umoja’s future. Recognition is based on a donor’s cumulative giving to the Home Ground campaign.

Home Ground Supporter$500–$2,499 Recognition
  • Optional recognition on the Home Ground campaign page and in digital donor recognition
Groundbreaker$2,500–$9,999 Recognition
  • Campaign-page recognition
  • Inclusion in the permanent Home Ground donor recognition installation once the campus exists
Builder$10,000–$24,999 Recognition
  • Permanent donor recognition
  • Invitation to appropriate Home Ground campaign milestones, such as groundbreaking and opening events
Cornerstone$25,000–$49,999 Recognition
  • Prominent permanent recognition
  • Cornerstone designation on the Home Ground page
  • Campaign progress and project updates
  • Invitations to key Home Ground milestones and site events
Pillar$50,000–$99,999 Recognition
  • Prominent permanent recognition
  • Enhanced placement within the Home Ground recognition system
  • Deeper campaign and project stewardship and updates
  • Invitations to key Home Ground milestones
Legacy$100,000+ Recognition
  • Top-level Home Ground recognition
  • Prominent permanent acknowledgement
  • Individualized campaign stewardship
  • An opportunity to discuss whether an appropriate major capital-asset recognition or naming opportunity exists

Home Ground is open to everyone. Individuals, families, businesses, foundations and organizations can all support the campaign. Supporting Home Ground does not make an organization an annual sponsor, and an annual sponsorship does not direct any money to Home Ground — an organization can choose to do both. A company seeking a major permanent campus logo, a named asset, field recognition or similar commercial recognition is a separately negotiated agreement rather than part of this ladder.

Recognition is cumulative. It reflects total charitable giving to the Home Ground campaign rather than any single gift. A donor who gives $10,000 and later gives another $15,000 has contributed $25,000 to Home Ground and reaches Cornerstone. This is separate from the Umoja Circle, which is based on annual giving to the club’s operating mission.

Legacy does not automatically include naming rights. Field, campus, building, entrance, pavilion and training-space naming, and other major naming inventory, remain separately negotiated and approved. Home Ground recognition is permanent acknowledgement and stewardship for people helping build the club’s home — it carries no advertising benefits, apparel or logo placement, category exclusivity, Official Partner designation or commercial activation rights. Those sit with Partners.
